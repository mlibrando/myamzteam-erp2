# AU verification against Sellerboard — findings

Generated 2026-07-09. Supersedes the earlier draft of this file, which carried two
numbers this run disproved (see *Corrections* at the bottom).

Generated numbers live in [reference/data/au_sellerboard_reconcile.md](reference/data/au_sellerboard_reconcile.md),
regenerated by `python -m sync.reconcile_au`.

## TL;DR

- **Step 1 gate passed.** Units and orders match Sellerboard month by month, and refunds
  match exactly (21/21). The dollar gap is currency, not scope. All three currency claims
  verified arithmetically.
- **A fourth fact the brief did not have: Sellerboard reports Amazon fees GST-inclusive.**
  Our pipeline splits AU's 10% GST into a bare `Tax` leaf routed to passthrough. Verified to
  four significant figures (9.99%–10.01%) on two independent leaves, every month. Without
  normalising this, the implied FX rate looks ~10% too high on exactly the fee lines, and the
  anchors refuse to agree.
- **Sellerboard converts per transaction date, not per month.** Within a month its refund lines
  agree with each other to four decimals, its shipment lines agree with each other, and the two
  families land on *different* rates. No single monthly rate is exact.
- **The workbook's 0.67 is wrong enough to matter.** Measured reference rate runs 0.6736–0.7227,
  median **0.6971**. Using a flat 0.67 would misstate May by ~7%.
- **Post-FX, four of six months reconcile.** Feb/Apr/May/Jun sit inside the FX band on every
  bucket bar one marginal Feb FBA flag. **Jan and Mar do not**, and are named below rather than
  absorbed.
- **cog reconciles.** Σ Δ vs `salesCosts` = **+$178.23** on ~$13.4k, **mixed-sign** — drift, not
  systematic. This is materially better than UK's same-signed +5.8%.
- **The cog double-conversion trap is guarded.** `GMAKER-3` lands at 44.45 AUD; the guard rejects
  the ~21.60 (inverted) and ~63.77 (doubled) failure modes.

## The GST discovery (not in the brief)

Anchoring the FX rate on fee lines produced two clusters exactly **1.10×** apart. That is AU's GST,
not FX.

**Hypothesis 1** — `Shipment.Tax` = 10% × (FBA per-unit fee + shipping chargeback):

| month | Shipment.Tax | 10% of base | Δ | implied |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-01 | 114.62 | 114.71 | −0.09 | 9.99% |
| 2026-02 | 79.31 | 79.30 | +0.01 | 10.00% |
| 2026-03 | 47.29 | 47.26 | +0.03 | 10.01% |
| 2026-04 | 48.64 | 48.61 | +0.03 | 10.01% |
| 2026-05 | 51.97 | 51.97 | −0.00 | 10.00% |
| 2026-06 | 60.86 | 60.93 | −0.07 | 9.99% |

**Hypothesis 2** — `ServiceFee.Tax` = 10% × (storage + disposal + long-term): 9.88%–10.00%, same six
months.

**Hypothesis 3** — the referral fee carries **no** GST, so Sellerboard's "Referral fee" needs no
adjustment. Confirmed: its implied rate already sits in the lower cluster.

Consequences:

- Our `Shipment.FBAPerUnitFulfillmentFee` and `ServiceFee.FBAStorageFee` are **GST-exclusive**;
  Sellerboard's equivalents are **GST-inclusive**. `our_buckets_aud()` grosses ours up by 1.10.
- **AU `Shipment.Tax` is GST on Amazon's fees, not sales tax.** The `Shipment.Tax → passthrough`
  rule was inherited from CA/UK, where that leaf is a facilitator tax on *sales*. It happens to be
  the right destination for AU (fee GST is an input tax credit, recoverable), but it is mapping the
  wrong thing conceptually and should be commented as such before anyone reasons from it.
- AU's actual sales-tax passthrough is `LowValueGoodsTax-Principal` (−268.26), which offsets
  `OurPriceTax` (+268.26) to the cent — a clean collect-and-remit wash on 9 transactions.

## Step 2 — the implied rate

Reference rate is taken from **content-insensitive anchors only** (refunds, which describe identical
events on both sides, and advertising, which comes from the Ads API entirely outside the SP-API
transaction set). Revenue and fees are the buckets under test; fitting the rate to them would absorb
a scope error into the rate and then report a clean residual.

| month | reference | shipment | refund | service | ads | \|ship−ref\| |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-01 | 0.6736 | 0.5910 | 0.6735 | 0.6116 | 0.6790 | **0.0827** ⚠ |
| 2026-02 | 0.6957 | 0.6896 | 0.6956 | 0.7005 | 0.7022 | 0.0061 |
| 2026-03 | 0.7038 | 0.6767 | 0.7039 | 0.7014 | 0.6997 | **0.0271** ⚠ |
| 2026-04 | 0.6985 | 0.7167 | 0.6984 | 0.6916 | 0.7083 | 0.0182 |
| 2026-05 | 0.7227 | 0.7254 | 0.7304 | 0.7236 | 0.7183 | 0.0027 |
| 2026-06 | 0.6886 | 0.6867 | — | 0.7064 | 0.7036 | 0.0019 |

Median reference rate **0.6971**; the workbook says 0.67.

**Granularity verdict: sub-monthly.** Within a month the refund anchors agree to four decimals
(0.7038 / 0.7040 in March) and the shipment anchors agree among themselves (0.6744 / 0.6776 / 0.6767),
yet the two families differ. Sellerboard converts at transaction date. A monthly rate therefore always
leaves a residual, which is why the FX band exists.

The band is `|ours_aud| × 0.02`, an **absolute rate tolerance**, deliberately not a spread fitted to the
observed disagreement — a fitted band grows to swallow whatever gap it is shown, which is the exact
failure mode the task warns against.

### 2026-01 — named: shipment scope

The shipment family implies 0.5910 while refunds say 0.6735 and ads 0.6790. That is a 12% gap; AUD/USD
does not move 12% inside a month against one family of transactions and not another. We carry 9 units and
~2,100 AUD of revenue Sellerboard never counted. Refund lines in the same month agree to +0.32 USD on a
−1,350 base, which is what proves the *rate* is right and the *content* is wrong.

### 2026-03 — UNRESOLVED, not absorbed

The shipment family sits 0.0271 (3.9%) below reference — right at the edge of what date-mix can explain,
so I will not claim it either way. What is ruled out: it is **not** a unit-count effect. March's single
extra unit is a zero-revenue free replacement (one of four across the period), so the revenue-bearing unit
gap is zero while revenue is −272 USD off.

### The +14-unit aggregate

Across six months we count 14 more units and $1,789 more USD revenue than Sellerboard. The quotient,
**$127.80 per extra unit**, is within 1.3% of Sellerboard's own average unit price of $129.44 — consistent
with whole units we count and it does not. But the per-month alignment is poor (March has +1 unit and −272
USD; May has −1 unit and −70 USD), so the aggregate agreement may be coincidence. Neither the order-time
(dre=false, 344 units) nor settlement-time (dre=true, 354 units) basis reproduces Sellerboard's 330.
**Named as an open scope difference; mechanism not established.**

## Step 5 — named, non-drift differences

1. **Inventory-loss gap, Σ −$705.39** (not $845 — that figure was an addition error, now
   machine-checked by `assert_net_reproduces`). `productCosts − salesCosts = costOfMissingReturns +
   missingFromInboundCosts`, verified identically true all six months. Sellerboard's `netProfit` deducts
   it; our `listTransactions`-derived cog cannot see it. **A source-of-data difference, not drift.**
   Per month: −144.45, −243.20, −117.70, −69.03, −30.99, −100.02.

2. **Net basis is `productCosts`.** `sales + amazonFeesTotal + productCosts + advertising +
   refundCostsTotal + shippingCost` reproduces `netProfit` **to the cent, all six months**. `salesCosts`
   overshoots by exactly the inventory-loss gap. An earlier brief asserted the reverse; the data does not
   support it. Now enforced by `assert_net_reproduces`, so a schema change fails loudly.

3. **Parser quirks.** Skip the `is_totals` row and the `status:"preparing"` stub. `refundCostsTotal` is
   **signed, not always negative** (Jun = +30.99, being a `Value of returned items` recovery with no
   offsetting refund). Sellerboard pads some `name` values with a trailing space (`"Subscription "`).
   `end` is the last day at 00:00 UTC, not the first of the next month.

4. **GST is now verified, in the sense that matters — and still not verified in the sense the brief
   meant.** We have proven the 10% on Amazon *fees*. Sellerboard exposes no GST/VAT line at all
   (`vatTotal`, `vatFacilitator`, `vatCosts` are 0), so *sales*-GST pass-through **cannot be verified
   against this target**. A clean net does not prove it. Record AU sales-GST as **inferred, not verified**.

5. **AU refund basis is untested.** `order_purchase_date` has **0 rows for AU**, so the posted-vs-purchase
   test cannot run; refunds fall back to postedDate regardless of `MARKETPLACE_REFUND_COGS_BASIS["AU"]`.
   The config entry is now commented to say so. Backfill via `getOrders` before trusting it.

6. **AU `pnl_monthly` must stay unmaterialised for now.** `sync.cogs --marketplace AU` writes cog in **USD**
   into a pipeline whose other buckets would be **AUD**. Those rows were removed. `cogs.py` now labels the
   row with `cog_currency(marketplace_id)` rather than a hardcoded `"USD"` and warns when
   `cog_needs_fx()` — a no-op for US/CA/UK today, but the mismatch is no longer invisible.

## Step 6 — CA workbook currency (read-only; no CA number changed)

The CA sheet's COGS column is **CAD**: every SKU is exactly `US × 1.350`.

| SKU | CA cog | US cog | CA/US | CA price | US price | price ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MBDBOX1 | 82.80 | 61.33 | 1.350 | 228.74 | 199.95 | 1.144 |
| GMAKER-3 | 41.53 | 30.76 | 1.350 | 182.98 | 159.95 | 1.144 |
| JU-L51X-ZVP2 | 9.86 | 7.30 | 1.351 | 55.48 | 48.50 | 1.144 |
| UP-EMO2-CQK5 | 5.04 | 3.73 | 1.351 | 22.82 | 19.95 | 1.144 |

The retail column is **neither** CAD nor USD — it is `US × 1.144`, an invented multiplier, so the retail
column cannot be used to type the sheet. The cost column can: 1.350 is the CAD markup.

**The dependency the task suspected is real, and sharper than expected. Sellerise CA is itself
mixed-currency:**

- Sellerise CA `revenue` ÷ our CAD `Principal` ≈ **1.00** → its revenue is **CAD**.
- Sellerise CA `cog` ÷ our **US (USD)** basis = **0.962**; ÷ the CA-sheet (CAD) basis = **0.745**.
  → its cog is **USD**.

So CA reconciles because `MARKETPLACE_COG_SOURCE_OVERRIDE` happens to put a USD cog against a USD cog,
inside a CAD revenue comparison. That was diagnosed as "the CA sheet has a fake ×1.35 markup". The markup
is real *and correct as CAD* — the sheet is simply denominated differently from Sellerise's cog field. The
override works for the right numeric reason and the wrong stated reason. Now recorded in
`MARKETPLACE_COG_CURRENCY`, so it is a documented dependency rather than a coincidence.

**Separate observation, not touched:** `pnl_monthly` holds CA cog = 10,749.76, which is exactly the
CA-sheet (US×1.35) value, i.e. **stale, pre-override**. `reconcile.py` computes cog in memory via
`compute_cog_by_basis`, so CA's reported figures are unaffected. Re-running `sync.cogs --marketplace CA`
would rewrite these rows. Flagged, deliberately not run.
